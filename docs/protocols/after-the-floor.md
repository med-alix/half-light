# After the floor

On XEN day 2,999 (about **24 December 2030**) AMP reaches 1. Contract by contract:

## XEN keeps minting, but small

At AMP = 1 the reward becomes **r ≈ log2(Δ) × term**. XEN still pays for waiting and for network growth, about 9,400 XEN for a max-term mint. Mints happen whenever the price beats the gas cost (the **dawn line**). XEN becomes a small, steady money with:

| Part | After the floor |
| --- | --- |
| Issuance | Mints paid for time and network growth |
| Production cost | Gas: mints happen only when reward × price > gas |
| Savings rate | Staking APY fixed at 2% (also new issuance) |
| Sinks | Burns: DBXen, other temples, the Granary |

Net supply change = mints + stake rewards − burns. When burns exceed the other two, XEN is net deflationary. That crossing can be tracked live.

## Old light keeps arriving

AMP is locked at claimRank, so mints started before the floor still pay their old AMP. The last mints with AMP > 1 mature around **April 2032**: a twilight of last stars.

## Rank becomes citizenship

claimRank still works at AMP 1 for the price of gas. A rank becomes proof of participation more than income.

## DBXen burns the reserve

A batch is still 2.5M XEN, now drawn from existing supply. The fee depends on gas, not on XEN's price. Emission only decays on cycles with burns, so a quiet forge pauses emission instead of ending it. With emission at about 1/20 of today, burns fall by (1/20)^ε.

## NXD follows the burns

Fewer burns mean fewer fees, less DXN bought, less NXD burned.

## The consequence

Every layer now depends on one question: **will holders keep burning from a finite XEN reserve?** The Granary keeps a reserve for exactly this, and the game gives burns a social reason. Both need to be running well before 2030.
